## All 20 Magic 8 Ball Answers

Every Magic 8 Ball contains a twenty-sided die with one phrase on each face. The standard set is split into three groups: ten affirmative, five noncommittal, and five negative.

### The 10 Affirmative Answers

1. **It is certain.** The strongest yes on the die.
2. **It is decidedly so.** Confident, slightly formal agreement.
3. **Without a doubt.** A yes with no hedging.
4. **Yes definitely.** The plain, enthusiastic yes.
5. **You may rely on it.** A reassurance rather than just an answer.
6. **As I see it, yes.** A yes with a polite "in my opinion".
7. **Most likely.** A probable yes, not a promise.
8. **Outlook good.** Leans positive, with the future still open.
9. **Yes.** Short and unqualified.
10. **Signs point to yes.** Encouraging, with a bit of mystery.

### The 5 Noncommittal Answers

11. **Reply hazy, try again.** Shake it and ask again.
12. **Ask again later.** Come back to the question another time.
13. **Better not tell you now.** The ball is keeping a secret.
14. **Cannot predict now.** The ball is declining to guess.
15. **Concentrate and ask again.** An instruction to re-focus and repeat the question.

### The 5 Negative Answers

16. **Don't count on it.** A gentle no.
17. **My reply is no.** The clearest no.
18. **My sources say no.** A no with a hint of authority.
19. **Outlook not so good.** A cautious no.
20. **Very doubtful.** A strong lean toward no.

These are the answers listed in Wikipedia's article on the [Magic 8 Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_8_Ball) and printed on the standard toy.

## What Are Your Odds? The Math Behind the Answers

Because the die has twenty faces and each face is equally likely to land against the window, the chances for a single shake follow directly from the counts:

| Type of answer | Number of faces | Chance on one shake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | 10 | 50% |
| Noncommittal | 5 | 25% |
| Negative | 5 | 25% |

A few things follow from this:

- **A definite yes is twice as likely as a definite no.** One shake gives you a yes half the time but a no only a quarter of the time.
- **Half of all shakes are not a yes.** That is why the Magic 8 Ball feels optimistic without being a sure thing.
- **Asking again is a trap.** If you keep shaking until you see a yes, you will eventually get one. Two shakes give you at least one yes about 75% of the time, and three shakes about 87.5%. The result then tells you nothing, because you chose when to stop.
- **A "no" or a "hazy" is not a failure of the ball.** Combined, they are half the die.

## What the Noncommittal Answers Are For

The five "hazy" answers are the most interesting part of the design. They are the toy's escape hatch. If your question was vague, unanswerable, or simply not a yes or no question, the ball has a graceful way to avoid giving a wrong answer. They also build suspense, because a hazy reply almost begs you to ask again.

In practice there is a useful lesson here. When a question gets a noncommittal answer, the real answer is usually that the question needs to be more specific. "Will my project go well?" is hazy. "Will I finish the draft by Friday?" can actually be answered.

## How to Ask a Good Question

The ball only knows yes, no, and maybe, so the way you phrase a question matters:

| Poor question | Why it fails | Better question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What should I eat tonight? | Not a yes or no question. | Should I order pizza tonight? |
| Will I be happy? | Too vague to answer. | Should I take the new job offer? |
| When will I get a reply? | The ball cannot give times. | Will I hear back this week? |
| Should I do A or B? | Two options, one answer. | Should I choose A? |

A few more tips: ask one question at a time, keep it short, and decide before you shake what a yes and a no will mean. That last habit matters most, because it stops you from reinterpreting an answer you do not like.

## Fun Ways to Use a Magic 8 Ball

The ball is more than a fortune-teller. A few games and uses that work well, with a real toy or an online version:

- **The prediction diary.** Ask the ball a question about something you can check later, such as whether it will rain on Saturday. Write down the answer, then record what actually happened. After a month you will have a clear, funny picture of how often a random answer is right, and a good lesson about chance.
- **Party game: "Ask the Oracle".** Each guest writes a harmless question, shakes the ball, and reads the answer aloud. Add a point for the funniest mismatch between question and reply.
- **Story starters.** Treat each answer as a plot twist. Ask a question about your character, then continue the story based on the result. The noncommittal answers make excellent cliffhangers.
- **Classroom probability.** Have students shake the ball twenty times, tally the three types of answer, and compare their results with the 50%, 25%, and 25% they should expect. It makes a vivid introduction to expected versus actual results. ## How the Ball Actually Works

Inside the sphere is a cylinder of dark blue liquid and a white twenty-sided die. When you turn the ball over, the die floats up and one face presses against a clear window. The letters are raised, so they push the dyed liquid aside and the message shows up in white on blue. There is no electronics and no connection between your question and the result. We cover the full story, including the liquid and the later design changes, in our article on the [history of the Magic 8 Ball](/blog/history-of-the-magic-8-ball/).

## A Quick History

The ball grew out of a spirit-writing device used by the mother of inventor Albert Carter. The toy's makers, who formed a company called Alabe Crafts, sold it first as a "Syco-Slate" and later in a crystal ball shape. In 1950, the pool-table maker Brunswick commissioned an eight-ball version, which became the shape everyone now recognizes. Mattel is the current maker. For the whole timeline, read our [history of the Magic 8 Ball](/blog/history-of-the-magic-8-ball/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many answers does a Magic 8 Ball have?

A Magic 8 Ball has twenty answers, one on each face of a twenty-sided die. Ten are affirmative, five are noncommittal, and five are negative, so a single shake gives a yes 50% of the time and a no 25% of the time.

### What are the three types of Magic 8 Ball answers?

The answers are affirmative, noncommittal, and negative. Affirmative answers include "It is certain" and "Signs point to yes". Noncommittal ones include "Reply hazy, try again" and "Ask again later". Negative ones include "Don't count on it" and "Very doubtful".

### Is the Magic 8 Ball random?

For practical purposes, yes. Which face ends up against the window depends on how the die floats and settles, and nothing about your question affects it. The answers themselves are not evenly balanced between yes and no, because ten of the twenty are affirmative.

### What should I do if the Magic 8 Ball says reply hazy?

A hazy answer usually means the question was unclear. Rephrase it as a specific yes or no question, then shake again. Do not keep asking the same question until you get the answer you want, because that makes the result meaningless.

### What is the most positive Magic 8 Ball answer?

"It is certain" is generally considered the strongest affirmative answer, followed by "It is decidedly so", "Without a doubt", and "Yes definitely". On the negative side, "My reply is no" is the clearest, with "Very doubtful" as a strong lean.
